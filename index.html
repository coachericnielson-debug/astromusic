Sounding now

    Reading the score

    bar lines
    Each vertical line starts a month in the year score and a day in the week and month views.
    ribbon width
    How fast the planet is moving across the sky. It thins as the planet slows, pinches to a thread at the station, then turns dark while it runs backward.
    The shadow: the stretch of sky the retrograde will cover, entered before it starts and left after it ends.
    fermata
    The hold sign marks a station, where the planet stands still before turning.
    Harmony rises above the line in teal (trines and sextiles). Tension hangs below in red (squares and oppositions). Gold is a conjunction, a meeting.
    contact lanes
    Each lane fades in as a transit approaches your planet and fades out as it leaves. A ring marks the exact moment. Three swells in one lane is a retrograde passing over the same point three times.

    Listening

    planet voices
    Tap a planet in the Voices row, or its name on the score, to hear it alone. Each planet is its own instrument in its own place left to right: the Sun a horn, the Moon a flute, Mercury quick ticks, Venus glass bells, Mars a growl, Jupiter an organ, Saturn a low cello, Uranus an electric trill, Neptune a haze, Pluto a heartbeat. Its pitch follows its speed, the way Kepler wrote each planet a melody in Harmonices Mundi (1619). Fast is high. Mercury's ticks, Mars's growl and Pluto's heartbeat also speed up and slow down with the planet. A station slides down an octave. Retrograde sounds muffled.
    aspect chords
    Each contact plays two tones set to its interval. Trines and sextiles are pure, sweet intervals (a fifth, a sixth). Squares and oppositions are buzzy and rub (a tritone, a minor ninth). Conjunctions are two voices on one note. Easy aspects start with a slow wobble that calms as they close and locks into harmony at exact. Hard aspects go the other way: the closer they get, the faster and harsher the rub, with a clashing half step added, and nothing resolves.
    major/minor bed
    A soft chord sits under everything. It is major while harmony leads, turns minor as tension grows, and goes diminished with a clashing half step when tension takes over. You can hear a hard week coming before you read about it.
    bells
    Bells ring when a contact is exact. An easy aspect rings a rising major chord that sounds finished. A hard aspect strikes three clashing bells at once that hang in the air unresolved. Deep gongs mark stations, and bright gold bells ring when two planets meet in the sky or the Moon turns new or full.
